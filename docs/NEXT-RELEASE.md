# Waiting for the next release

Codex and Freebuff: add one line per change you pushed. Write it in plain
words for the owner, and put your name in brackets. Claude moves these lines
into the next `docs/RELEASE-x.y.z.md` when it releases, then clears this list.

- Android phone agent: drag a control or screenshot point onto another (reorder, sliders, maps), and scroll until a named control is on screen; both refuse risky endpoints. iPhone: its own “On your phone” list and the best installed speaking voice. [Freebuff]
- Phone: the orb stops cutting listening off, the natural voice is kept whenever it can be, questions about today's news or events really search the web (and say so when they can't), and a thinking indicator shows a reply is on its way. [Freebuff]
- Phone: a PC screen answer says “PC screen captured” only when the PC really took a fresh frame of its screen while it answered, “PC screen not verified” when it cannot tell, and nothing at all when the PC never answered — never a claim without a receipt. [Freebuff]
- Phone: when something goes wrong the app now writes down why, where it used to stay quiet — a refused location fix, a map service that wouldn't answer, an orb Android turned down, a search Google refused — and Settings → “Something wrong?” copies it under **Now**. Nothing new is sent anywhere; it is there so the maker can read it. [Freebuff]
- Phone: Settings → Your orb has one **Orb colour** now, and every orb actually takes it — the orb on your screen, the floating orb over other apps (drawn natively, it had four fixed palettes before) and the preview in Settings — while resting, listening, thinking, blinking and talking. Drive mode keeps its Type button, so “Where to?” can be answered by typing when you are parked. [Freebuff]
- Drive mode rides along like a car's own co-pilot. Once you name a destination it measures the trip itself from location fixes and shows a trip line (to where, how far covered, how many minutes), answers “how's the drive going?” with those real numbers, and speaks at real moments: a check-in on a long leg, a word when you are crawling, and a nudge when a long stop looks like arrival. It never invents your route or an ETA — Google Maps still owns those — and Settings → Drive mode has a switch to turn the talking off. [Freebuff]
- Drive mode can finally say what's left. Once you name a destination the place is looked up on OpenStreetMap and routed from where you are, so the trip line shows the distance and drive time left, “how long is left” answers with the real numbers, and a nearly-there line joins the check-in, crawl and arrival nudges. It says the time is the road's usual drive time, not live traffic, and when the lookup or the router can't answer — a place it can't find, no signal, “home” with no saved address — it says exactly that instead of guessing. [Freebuff]
- Izuki now keeps its own notes about what kept going wrong: a small local list (`%APPDATA%\Izuki\agent-notes.jsonl`), never shown in the app and never sent anywhere, written from the same scrubbed facts as the error reports. Whoever fixes Izuki next — including a coding agent — can run `node tools/agent-notes.cjs` and see what breaks most, in which part of the app, on which build, instead of asking. [Freebuff]
- Phone answers that used Google's search now show the pages behind them: title and a working link, only the ones the search really returned, under the honest "N sources came back" line. [Freebuff]
- Drive mode’s trip now outlives the screen. While Google Maps has the front, the floating orb carries what’s left (“12 min · 3.9 mi to Walmart”) instead of a blank orb, renewed as you drive so it does not time out; if Android closes Izuki mid-drive, the destination and the distance covered come back on the next launch and the route is asked for again from where you are, so yesterday’s time is never shown as today’s. The orb line follows the “keep the glass orb over other apps” setting. [Freebuff]
- **New: Izuki in VR (preview).** Open izukinova.ai/vr in a headset's browser (Meta Quest, Pico, Android XR, Vision Pro, or a PC headset) and go in: the IZUKI NOVA orb floats in your real room (or a calm space) and follows you gently; point at it and pull the trigger (or pinch) to talk. It's the same Izuki, with your brain, memory, voice and PC link. Say “what's this?” and, on headsets that share their camera (Quest 3/3S, after your yes), it looks, answers and circles what it means on the photo; a graded test gets coaching, not answers. Say “show my PC” for your PC's screen, big, in the room: point and click on it, thumbstick to scroll, and watch Izuki work there, with Stop. “Pin that” keeps an answer as a card you can carry anywhere. A normal screen gets a 3D preview. Not yet tried on a real headset. [Claude]
- **Izuki VR as a Quest app.** A small app (built by Meta's own packaging tool) puts Izuki VR in your Quest's library: its icon opens Izuki and goes straight into your room once you're set up. Install it by sideloading (Developer Mode + USB, or SideQuest); it shows under Unknown Sources. Not yet installed on a real headset. [Claude]
