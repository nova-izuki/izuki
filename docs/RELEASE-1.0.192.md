# Izuki 1.0.192: nothing lost when the laptop wakes up

## 📬 Messages to your phone wait for the internet

When a laptop wakes up, Wi-Fi takes a moment to come back, and Izuki used to be quicker than it. Waking at 1:51 am, it sent eight school heads-ups to Discord in the first second, every one failed with "couldn't send", and every one was gone.

Now a message that couldn't leave (no connection at all) waits and goes as soon as the internet is back, in the order it was written. If it waited more than a couple of minutes, it says when it was from: *"(From 1:51 AM, while I was offline) …"*. A message the server did receive is never sent again, so nothing arrives twice. Old news isn't sent at all: anything that waited over six hours is dropped. This covers Discord and Telegram, for heads-ups, reminders and "done" messages alike.

## 📚 One heads-up for everything due, not eight

Things due at the same time now come as one heads-up: *"8 things due soon"*, with the first six listed and "ask me *what's due this week?*" for the rest. Course names read the way you'd say them: **"Security Management Practices (ITSY-2341)"** instead of "ITSY-2341-372-202710-14278: 202710:14278 Security Management Practices (ITSY-2341-372)". Asking about "section 372" still finds the course.

## 🗣️ It says what it actually did

On a quiz page, the screen helper planned to pick the answers, the quiz guard stopped it, and a scroll still ran. While that happened, Izuki said the plan's own line out loud: *"Answering all the quiz questions for you."* When a step is held back, Izuki now says what really happened instead: it doesn't pick quiz answers, and it can explain the question.

## 🕑 Routines and reminders on the night the clocks change

The clocks went back an hour on 1 November. That night 1:30 am happens twice, and in March 2:30 am doesn't happen at all, and Izuki gave up on both: a daily routine set inside that hour was skipped for the day, and a reminder for it was dropped. Now it runs once, at the first 1:30 in autumn and at 3:30 in spring. The morning brief and calendar times use the same rule.
