# Izuki 1.0.191: keep your PC awake while something runs

## ☕ "Keep my PC awake"

A laptop that goes to sleep takes whatever was running with it: a big download, an export, a render — or a coding assistant working while you're away, which is exactly how a long piece of work stopped mid-check here.

Now you can say:

- **"Keep my PC awake for 3 hours"** — or 30 minutes, an hour and a half, "2h30m", "overnight"
- **"Keep it awake"** — until you say otherwise, 12 hours at most
- **"Let it sleep"** — back to normal straight away
- **"Are you keeping my PC awake?"** — how long is left

Only the PC is kept awake, not the screen: the display still turns off on its usual timer, and locks if Windows is set to lock when it does. **Closing the lid or pressing the power button still puts it to sleep**, so on a laptop, leave the lid open (or set what closing it does in Windows' power settings). If you're on battery it tells you, and at 30% or less it asks you to plug in. It never holds on forever — twelve hours at the most — and a fresh request replaces the old one.

It's careful about what it hears: *"help me stay awake while I study"* is about you, not the PC, so it's left to the chat, and so is *"how do I keep my laptop awake?"*: a question gets an answer, not a twelve-hour hold. *"Don't let it sleep"* holds sleep off (an earlier draft read the "let it sleep" inside it and did the opposite).

## Browser panel: "ans all" (Freebuff)

Typing **"ans all"** (or "answer all", "ans all please") in the side panel on a quiz page now starts the explain-each-question run, the same as "answer all these questions". Before, the short way of saying it went to the desktop task handoff instead. As with every quiz run, Izuki explains each question and suggests an answer with the reason; you choose and move on yourself. "Answer all emails" is still not a quiz, and neither is a bare "answer these": that's as likely to be about questions you just pasted into the chat.
