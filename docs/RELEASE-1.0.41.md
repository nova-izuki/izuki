# Izuki 1.0.41

Izuki TV with full control of Android TVs, one setup for your PC, phone and TV, a Later list, and Izuki's voice from the TV.

## Izuki TV — full control
Open the Izuki app on an Android TV, Google TV or Fire TV and it becomes a big-screen companion: a realistic orb with a soft reflection, the time, what you said and what Izuki says in large type, and big tiles the remote moves between (it dims into a screensaver when nothing's happening).
- **Two ways to talk**, switchable on screen: say **"Hey Nova"** (it only answers when it hears its name, so the TV's own sound doesn't set it off), or **hold OK** on the remote and talk while a live waveform shows it's hearing you.
- **It runs the TV.** "Open Netflix", "pause", "turn it down", "next episode" happen instantly. Turn on **Control this TV** once (Android's Accessibility switch) and it can also read what's on the screen and press, type and scroll — "find Stranger Things and play it" opens the app, searches, picks the show and presses play, step by step, saying what it's doing.
- A small Izuki orb floats over Netflix and other apps while it listens or talks.
- Nine orb looks for the TV, including the new Aurora and Nebula.

## One setup for everything
Turn on **Let my phone and TV link to this PC** (Settings → Control my TV). The TV app finds the PC on your Wi-Fi; the phone uses the Call Izuki link it already has ("Copy my PC's setup"). A box asks you on the PC — with the same code the device shows — and on **Allow** the device copies your AI keys, your connected apps (Composio — "check my Gmail" works from the phone), your character, language and what Izuki remembers. Then it works on its own, even with the PC off. Linked devices are listed on the PC, with Unlink.
The PC also hands TV jobs to a linked TV app ("Hey Nova, open YouTube on the TV"), and when the TV hears "Hey Nova" the PC stays quiet, so they never both answer.

## Later list
"Remind me later I'm buying Scrubbing Bubbles and Sensodyne" — no time needed. Each thing lands on your **Later list** (top of the Chat tab, with checkboxes), and Izuki brings it up by itself when you're back at the PC, in the morning and before the shops close. "What's on my list?", "add milk to my list", "I got the toothpaste" all work — from voice, the chat, the phone and Telegram.

## Izuki talks from the TV
With the Izuki channel open on a Roku, Izuki's voice now plays from the TV's own speakers (switchable: **Talk from the TV**), and the TV orb can have its own look — glass orbs tinted to match, plus Aurora and Nebula that drift through their colours.

## A status screen you can use
"Hey Nova, wake up" now brings up a live, hands-on status screen: the clock ticks, and CPU, memory, battery, free space and your connection update every two seconds. Tap a gauge and Izuki checks what's using it (in the background); tap an email to have it read with what you need to do; tap a meeting for who's in it and what to prepare; tap a linked app for what's new; play, pause and skip your music; tick off your Later list. An "Ask" bar and one-tap actions (plan my day, tidy my PC, today's news, weather, music) sit along the bottom, and the screen stays up while you use it.

## Catches its own misclicks
If a click slips onto the taskbar and opens Windows Search or the Start menu by mistake, Izuki notices, closes it and aims again — instead of carrying on in the wrong place.
